## What it is

A **mandate** is a power of representation, signed by someone with authority, that says **who the agent works for, what it may do, on what, and up to how much**. Inside it the agent acts alone; outside it, it asks a person.

## Why you need it on top of what you already have

A policy that asks a person every time makes the agent useless: it is a very slow form. A blanket permission makes it dangerous. And a rule written in your code ("up to 5,000 EUR") has no author: nobody signed it, nobody can show later who granted it or when it changed.

A mandate is the middle ground **with a signature**. Say the finance department grants the executor agent: *"place orders for Carlos, in one portfolio, up to 5,000 EUR and 100 shares each"*. The executor sends a 1,000 EUR order alone. A 6,000 EUR order stops and asks a person of finance. An agent with no mandate sends nothing. And the audit shows **which power** covered each order, who issued it and for whom.

A role says who may **approve**. A mandate says what the agent may **do alone**. They answer different questions and work together.

## How it works here

1. **An issuer** (your finance department, an identity service) signs a credential for the agent: actions, resources, limits, expiry.
2. **Before a call the mandate applies to**, the policy asks a **verifier** to turn the credential into a `Mandate`, or refuse it (unknown issuer, expired, revoked, bad signature).
3. **The policy compares the call with the mandate**: is the action listed, the resource covered, the amount and every other cap within its maximum, is the credential for this agent and this person?
4. **Inside** → the call runs and the audit event carries `mandate`. **Outside** → it asks a person (`outOfScope: 'ask'`, optionally restricted to a role with `outOfScopeApprover`) or is denied. **No valid mandate** → nothing runs.

## Where it applies: only to what is critical

A mandate is worth keeping for what **cannot be undone**: moving money, deleting data, restarting or reconfiguring a system, granting access, publishing, signing for the person. Reading, planning and handing work over do not need a signed power.

`mandatePolicy({ appliesTo: tool => tool.destructive === true })` says which tools it governs; the rest go to the framework's own policy (a read runs). Forgetting a critical tool there is annoying, never permissive: it falls to that policy and asks.

What a mandate says is the same in any business; only the nouns change:

| Agent | What it may do | On what | A cap that is not money |
|---|---|---|---|
| Support | `send_email`, `issue_refund` | customers of one region | recipients per message |
| Code | `edit_file`, `merge` | paths under `src/` | files changed per operation |
| Operations | `restart_service` | the `staging` environment | instances restarted at once |
| Data | `delete_rows` | one table | rows deleted per operation |
| Trading | `place_order` | one portfolio | shares per order, besides the amount |

## Details that matter

- **The maximum is per operation.** `limits.measures` caps **one call**: rows per delete, recipients per message. A cap the policy cannot measure leaves the call outside the mandate (fail closed).
- **Counters and calendars are yours.** "Up to 50 emails a day" or "only on weekdays" need state or a clock; they belong to your agent's business logic, not to a signed power.
- **The credential can be tied to the agent's key** (`holderBinding`). A copy of the credential in another agent's hands is useless without the key. Say you require it with `requireHolderBinding: true`; it is off by default, because it needs credentials issued that way.
- **Fail closed everywhere.** A restriction the framework cannot enforce is refused, never ignored: a credential that says something the verifier does not understand does not become "no limit".
- **Who may take the responsibility of going past it** is the [approval](/concepts/approval/) rule: a role of the directory, never the person who asked.
- **The format is replaceable.** `agent-core` knows only the contract; [`agent-mandates`](/packages/mandates/) brings a reference verifier, and you can write an adapter for another provider. Your agent, the policy and the audit do not change when you swap it.

## What it does not do

- It does **not** decide who the agent is: that is the [identity](/concepts/agent-identity/).
- It does **not** count over time or schedule anything (see above).
- It does **not** replace approval: outside the mandate someone still answers.
- The reference credential is a profile of our own; an official format from an identity service is still to be adapted.

## In one line

A mandate is **a signed limit on what the agent may do alone**, kept for what is critical; everything beyond it asks a person.
